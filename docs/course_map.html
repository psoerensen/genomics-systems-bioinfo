Unified course map

Reusable building blocks for genomics, systems biology, bioinformatics and multi-omics

One framework, reusable teaching blocks

Genomics, Systems Biology and Bioinformatics is the unified teaching framework spanning both harmonized MSc outlines: Genomics, Systems Biology and Bioinformatics and Multi-Omics Technology in Animal Production. The two outlines define different pathways through the same library of material. The unified course can also select its own sequence from that library.

Each block has one maintained explanation and worked example. A pathway chooses its depth, exercises and assessment. Shared content is revisited through a new question or a more demanding analysis when students take both courses.

This map is a curriculum design. Only the overview and this map are currently implemented; the lessons, practicals and assessments below are proposed. Original module numbering is retained in the crosswalks. Partner assignments, credit hours and assessment weights remain as specified in the supplied outlines; this map does not revise them or allocate teaching hours.

Building blocks

The block numbers are stable identifiers, rather than a compulsory teaching order. Technologies can be introduced alongside the data and design decisions that make their measurements interpretable.

Block Scope Students should be able to Proposed worked example or practical
B01 Foundations and biological questions Genomics, systems biology, bioinformatics; omics layers; genotype–phenotype relationships Define a biological question and identify the measurements needed to address it Map a feed-efficiency question to genomic, expression, microbial and phenotype measurements
B02 Experimental design and sampling Experimental units, biological replication, tissue and time, matched samples, collection and preservation, batch allocation Specify a sampling design and distinguish biological effects from confounding Compare matched and unmatched multi-omics designs; diagnose a treatment–batch confounding problem
B03 Databases and biological identifiers Sequence, protein, structure, genome, comparative, annotation and pathway resources; identifier mapping Retrieve and document records while preserving identifier meaning and annotation provenance Trace a variant to a gene, transcript, protein and pathway using a small documented example
B04 Programming and reproducible data handling R; optional command-line/Python extensions; file formats, joins, sequence manipulation and workflow organisation Import data, verify sample identity and produce a reproducible analysis Join phenotype and assay tables; detect swapped samples, missing identifiers and duplicate records
B05 Genomics and sequence analysis Genome organisation, sequencing, genotyping, variation, assembly and annotation concepts; sequence similarity, alignment and phylogeny Explain genomic measurements and evaluate basic sequence/genotype quality Inspect sequence and genotype data; compare homologous sequences with explicit assumptions
B06 Transcriptomics RNA-seq technologies, expression quantification, quality control, normalization, differential expression and functional interpretation Explain an expression analysis and its design, contrasts and uncertainty Analyse a small count dataset with biological replicates and a stated experimental contrast
B07 Epigenomics DNA methylation, chromatin measurements, nutritional/environmental influences and regulatory interpretation Interpret epigenomic measurements without equating association with causation Compare regulatory measurements across tissues or conditions and identify alternative explanations
B08 Proteomics and structural information Platforms, identification, quantification, protein structures and functional interpretation Explain protein measurements and assess identification and quantification limitations Explore a protein-abundance table with missing values and batch information
B09 Metabolomics Analytical platforms, identification confidence, quantification, preprocessing and biochemical interpretation Evaluate metabolite measurements and distinguish identified compounds from unresolved features Examine a small metabolite panel, measurement scales and feature-identification uncertainty
B10 Microbiomics and host–microbiome relationships Community profiling, host effects, co-metabolism, compositionality and production applications Interpret community differences while accounting for sampling and measurement constraints Compare rumen-community profiles with diet and host metadata; discuss relative versus absolute abundance
B11 Phenomics and precision production Sensors, repeated measurements, high-throughput phenotyping and links to omics Define meaningful phenotypes and account for measurement timing and repeated records Align animal-level sensor summaries with biological sampling dates
B12 Statistical analysis and validation Regression, multivariate analysis, multiple testing, clustering, dimension reduction, regularization and machine learning Select an analysis and evaluate uncertainty, overfitting, confounding and leakage Fit and compare predictive models with animal-level train/test separation and preprocessing fitted on training data
B13 Association, prediction and regulatory variation GWAS, relatedness, population structure, fine-mapping, functional annotation, genomic prediction, eQTL and TWAS Distinguish association, prediction and causal claims and explain their assumptions Interpret an association region and expression evidence; assess prediction on an appropriate held-out group
B14 Pathways, networks and systems inference Enrichment, gene regulation, protein interactions, metabolic/signalling networks and causal-model concepts Evaluate pathway/network evidence, background sets, uncertainty and limits of inference Compare enrichment backgrounds and assess whether a network supports a biological hypothesis
B15 Multi-omics integration Integration strategies, sample matching, scaling, missing layers, cross-layer association and genotype-to-phenotype integration Justify an integration strategy and interpret agreement and disagreement between layers Integrate matched expression, protein and phenotype summaries with a documented design and validation plan
B16 Advanced and emerging methods Single-cell, spatial omics, advanced epigenomics, 3D genome organisation and emerging AI methods Evaluate what a new technology measures and whether it answers the study question Critique an advanced-omics paper, separating measurement, association and mechanistic evidence
B17 Ethics, governance and research integrity Privacy/security, sharing, open science, provenance, reproducibility and responsible AI Specify responsible data handling and communicate analysis provenance and limitations Write a data-management and reproducibility plan, including justified AI use
B18 Animal-production synthesis and reporting Growth, feed efficiency, meat/milk quality, reproduction, health, adaptation, methane and sustainability Connect a justified study design and analysis to an animal-production question Develop an integrated case study, computational report or research proposal

B02, B04 and B17 recur throughout the course. B12 supports analyses across technologies. B18 provides applications throughout the teaching sequence, as well as a final synthesis.

Shared lessons and different extensions

Each shared lesson should have a clear entry requirement, a core explanation, a worked example, exercises with solutions and optional extensions. Reuse the same lesson when its learning objective is the same. Assign a different exercise when the objective or depth differs.

Example Shared core GSB pathway extension Multi-Omics pathway extension
RNA-seq Experimental units, count data, quality control, normalization and contrasts Model design, uncertainty and statistical interpretation Tissue/time selection, complementary assays and integration with protein measurements
Genomic prediction Genotypes, phenotypes, relatedness and appropriate validation Model comparison, population structure and limits of transfer Assess whether extra omics measurements add useful information at the intended decision time
Pathway enrichment Gene identifiers, eligible background, multiple testing and interpretation Sensitivity to background choice and analysis assumptions Compare pathway evidence across layers without treating agreement as independent replication
Networks Nodes, edges, construction assumptions and uncertainty Compare network methods and distinguish association from causal evidence Interpret host–microbiome or molecular relationships in a production-trait study
Integration Matched samples, compatible identifiers/scales, missing layers and validation Compare statistical integration strategies under stated assumptions Justify technology and sampling choices and interpret the integrated biological evidence

An advanced extension should not quietly become a prerequisite for an introductory lesson. Students taking both pathways can reuse the core lesson and demonstrate a new competence in the extension.

Learning outcomes and assessment

The GSB outline emphasises applying computational methods, explaining association and networks, integrating data, and evaluating quality and biological significance. B03–B06 and B12–B15 provide the main analytical spine; B17–B18 support evaluation and reporting.

The Multi-Omics outline explicitly assesses distinguishing technologies, explaining collection/preservation, interpreting genomic/transcriptomic data and evaluating animal-production applications. B01–B02, B05–B11 and B18 directly support those stated outcomes. Its content also includes substantial integration and analysis in MO10–11; proposed additional outcomes would ask students to design a multi-omics study, evaluate data quality and confounding, and justify and interpret an integration strategy. These additions should be agreed before being presented as formal course requirements.

The original assessment weights are preserved:

Shared practicals can use a common core rubric: correct sample/variable definitions, justified methods, reproducibility and interpretation. Pathway extensions assess additional competencies. A GSB computational report can emphasise method evaluation; a Multi-Omics proposal can emphasise sampling, technology choice and integration design. Neither project form replaces the original assessment scheme without agreement.

Suggested pathways through the framework

These are teaching sequences, not allocations of credit hours. Each course can select a subset appropriate to its available time.

Unified course: introduce B01, B02, B04 and B17; survey B05–B11 alongside B03; build the analytical foundation in B12; develop B13, B14 and B15; finish with B18 and selected B16 extensions. Animal-production examples recur throughout.

GSB pathway: B01 → B03/B04 → selected B05–B07 → B12 → B13 → B14/B15 → selected B16 → synthesis and reporting. Introduce design and reproducibility as needed throughout, and use B08–B11 where they support the selected questions.

Multi-Omics pathway: B01/B02 → B05–B11 → B03/B04/B12 → B14/B15 → B18. Revisit study design before integration and use selected B13 lessons for prediction and genotype-to-phenotype relationships.

R is the primary environment for shared computational examples, consistent with the existing teaching sites. Command-line and Python exercises can be optional extensions where the original bioinformatics pathway calls for them. Software choices and dataset size should be specified when each practical is developed.

Development and reuse

Keep this repository as the shared home. Use existing slides/, notes/, tutorials/, exercises/, data/, images/, R/ and scripts/ folders, with each resource labelled by its block identifier and linked from this map as it becomes available. Avoid copying the same explanation into separate course versions. Both pathways can link to one resource and assign different extensions.

Develop one complete example first: B02/B04/B06, a small RNA-seq study with sample metadata, quality checks and a biological contrast. Follow it with B12/B14 interpretation and then a B15 integration example. This connects design, computation and biological interpretation without requiring all eighteen blocks to be written first.

Existing Population Genetics, Quantitative Genetics and Bayesian Linear Regression lessons can supply prerequisites or optional background. Link to suitable lessons after checking their assumptions and level. Package integrations should use current public interfaces and should not be prerequisites until an example has been implemented and verified.
