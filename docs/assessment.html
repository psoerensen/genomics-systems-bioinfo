Learning outcomes and assessment

An assessment should reveal whether you can choose and defend an analysis, not only reproduce code. This guide connects the shared modules to observable evidence of learning. Its assignments and rubrics are suggested teaching resources; the formal component weights in the course map remain unchanged.

Three case assignments

Case 1: A paired transcriptomic response

Use the airway case. Submit a 1,000-word report, two figures and the analysis script. State the treated-minus-untreated contrast. Explain why cell line belongs in the design. Report effect sizes and adjusted p-values for a prespecified gene and an exploratory top-ranked gene. Compare the paired model with an unpaired sensitivity analysis; interpret any disagreement in light of the design. Explain what the experiment cannot establish about patient response.

Suggested reasoning: removing the cell-line term changes the residual variation and the comparison model; it does not manufacture four extra independent donors. A small adjusted p-value supports a response under the model, while transfer to patients needs separate evidence. A missing adjusted p-value is not an estimate of no effect.

Case 2: What does an absent protein intensity mean?

Use the proteomics case. Submit a missingness display, the Ubi4-versus-control result table and a 750-word interpretation. Compare the primary observed-value analysis with its stricter complete-case sensitivity analysis. Explain why a protein found only in enriched samples is excluded from that primary contrast. Propose a follow-up measurement without treating imputed intensities as observed values.

Suggested reasoning: a detection pattern can be biologically interesting but cannot supply an observed mean and sampling variance in a group with no measurements. Filtering changes the population of proteins being assessed. An independent enrichment assay or targeted measurement can address that gap; claiming absence from cells would exceed an affinity-enrichment experiment.

Case 3: Disease-associated layers in cattle

Use the cattle case. Submit an identity audit, a covariate balance table, three exploratory figures and a 1,250-word report. Compare pooled and disease-adjusted cross-layer relationships, and unadjusted versus covariate-adjusted metabolite associations. Define exactly which animals and features contribute. Finish with a sampling plan for a prospective study.

Suggested reasoning: a pooled RNA–metabolite correlation can be created by both assays differing between disease groups. Adjustment may weaken it without showing that the pooled calculation was numerically wrong. Concurrent case-control measurements can identify disease-associated patterns but do not establish a marker available before illness or a transferable prediction threshold.

Synthesis assignment

Question: An animal-production team wants to use molecular measurements to improve a health or production decision. What can the present evidence support, and what should the team measure next?

Choose one real case as your empirical anchor. Use the other cases or existing hypothetical practicals to compare methods, explicitly identifying their different designs and scales. Do not merge observations from unrelated studies into a purported matched multi-omics dataset.

Submit a report of approximately 2,000 words, an executable script, a sample/measurement diagram and no more than four main figures. Include:

  1. A question and intended decision time, with the target population and estimand.
  2. A sample identity/QC audit, including every exclusion and its consequence.
  3. A justified primary method and one reasonable alternative or sensitivity analysis.
  4. A result that combines effect sizes, uncertainty and biological measurement limits.
  5. At least one apparent disagreement: between methods, layers, or pooled and adjusted evidence. Explain plausible reasons and what would discriminate between them.
  6. A practical follow-up plan, specifying sampling, replication, assay choice and independent validation.

For the GSB pathway, emphasize computational choices, diagnostics and model comparison. For Multi-Omics, emphasize technology, sampling/preservation and the production decision. Both must demonstrate the shared core; these extensions do not alter the original formal assessment weights.

Suggested 100-point rubric

Criterion Points Strong evidence A limitation that reduces credit
Question and design 15 Explicit estimand, units, timing and target population A vague question or wrong replication unit
Identity and measurement quality 20 Auditable joins, units, exclusions and assay limits Joining by row order; unexplained exclusions
Method choice and assumptions 20 Justified contrast/model, diagnostic and alternative Software named without explaining its assumptions
Evidence and disagreement 20 Effect sizes, uncertainty, multiplicity and reasoned sensitivity Ranking by p-value alone or ignoring conflicting evidence
Interpretation and follow-up 15 Bounded biological conclusion and feasible discriminating study Causal/predictive claim beyond the design
Reproducibility and communication 10 Script runs, provenance/version record, clear figures Missing inputs, undocumented manual edits or unreadable plots
Total 100

Suggested performance bands: excellent work makes the reasoning auditable and investigates an alternative; sound work gives a correct bounded analysis but a limited sensitivity investigation; developing work reproduces calculations with weak justifications; insufficient work has a material identity/design error or an unsupported central conclusion. Institutions should apply their own grading scale.

A reusable feedback protocol

Before submission, exchange the one-page analysis record with a peer. Ask the peer to identify the observation unit, explain the contrast without reading the code, and locate the strongest unsupported sentence. Revise that sentence or supply the missing evidence. After receiving feedback, record one analysis decision you changed and why.

For instructors, return feedback against the criteria above, distinguishing an incorrect calculation from a defensible alternative method. Keep suggested answers hidden during initial work. If AI assistance is permitted, require a short record of its role and the checks the student performed; polished prose is not evidence that the student can defend the analysis.
